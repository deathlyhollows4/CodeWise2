# CodeWise2: learning basis and student motivations

Research date: 2026-10-12. Research only; no learners contacted, purchases, surveys, application changes, or runtime validation. App facts below are coordinator-supplied context, not independently inspected. Runtime model/effort was requested by the coordinator but effective settings are not visible to this research agent; no speed claim is made.

## Decision

**Inference:** The defensible learning proposition is “Turn helped practice into evidence that you can solve a fresh problem yourself.” A subscription must earn its price through reliable sequencing, review, quality feedback and demonstrable transfer. Access to more answers or more AI is an insufficient educational distinction. Effectiveness and willingness to pay remain hypotheses.

The supplied context—prerequisite adaptation, assistance tags, a fresh same-step checkpoint and reviews after 1/3/7/14/30 days—fits this mechanism. Those features do not establish that it works. The exact review schedule has not been validated by the studies below. Own-key DeepSeek is unvalidated; saved AI and local-first operation are product choices, not proven learning interventions. A 243-question public bank and three languages do not establish depth, checker accuracy, transfer difficulty, or broad platform access; Windows ARM64/WSL verification does not establish global readiness.

**Correction verification:** The published PNAS correction (August 20, 2025) changes Osbert Bastani's institutional affiliation because of a production error; it does not amend results. The actual one-page correction was opened through a PDF mirror after PMC's browser check blocked direct access. This supports retaining the directional finding below, rather than silently disregarding a correction. [Published correction PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/4e8b/adc5aef00eef42a935570827ec170420106d.pdf), [PubMed correction record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40833419/).

## Evidence and its limits

1. **Fact: AI performance gains can coexist with weaker unaided outcomes.** Bastani et al. studied nearly 1,000 Turkish high-school mathematics students. Unrestricted GPT access improved practice performance but reduced later unaided grades relative to control; a teacher-informed hints tutor largely mitigated that harm. **Inference:** Score assisted work separately and require fresh unaided evidence. **Limit:** This is mathematics, not college DSA, and does not demonstrate that CodeWise2 prevents dependence. The published article records a correction; the accessible author PDF was opened as well. [Published record](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), [author paper](https://hamsabastani.github.io/education_llm.pdf).

2. **Fact: programming evidence is mixed, rather than uniformly anti-AI.** A controlled study of 69 novices aged 10–17 using Codex on Python tasks improved authoring completion and scores without reducing manual modification performance. Its one-week post-test advantage was not statistically significant. **Inference:** Do not market “AI makes you worse”; assess design and baseline skill. **Limit:** Small youth sample, introductory programming and an older model. [Kazemitabaar et al., CHI 2023 author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07427).

3. **Fact: approach to assistance matters.** A thematic analysis of 33 young novices identified whole-solution, stepwise, hybrid and manual approaches. Whole-solution prompting had the highest authoring correctness and lowest subsequent modification correctness during training. **Inference:** Assistance tags should identify how help was used; completion is a poor mastery proxy. **Limit:** Observational associations do not establish causality or adult DSA effects. [Koli Calling 2023 author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14049).

4. **Fact: cognitive engagement techniques have direct programming relevance.** Kazemitabaar et al. evaluated techniques in studies with 82 and 42 participants, including isomorphic tasks without AI and calibration of perceived versus actual ability. Their strongest reported technique required learners to propose each step before revealing code. **Inference:** Ask for a plan/prediction before progressively revealing help. **Limit:** This opened source is an author preprint; isomorphic transfer is narrower than unfamiliar interview problems. [2024 author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08922).

5. **Fact: retrieval can outperform additional elaborative study.** Karpicke and Blunt's experiments with science texts found benefits on comprehension and inference tests, including concept-map tests. **Inference:** Review should require reconstruction—explain an invariant, trace a case, choose an approach or implement—before showing the answer. **Limit:** Text learning does not prove executable DSA skill gains. [Science 2011 author PDF](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf).

6. **Fact: spacing depends on retention goals.** Cepeda et al. taught facts to over 1,350 participants with reviews and tests extending over months. Optimal gaps changed with the final-test delay. **Inference:** 1/3/7/14/30 is a plausible starting policy, not a scientifically optimal universal schedule; adjust around demonstrated recall and placement dates. **Limit:** Fact retention differs from problem solving. [Institutional primary-paper record](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1766/).

7. **Fact: fading examples supports a transition to independent solving.** Renkl, Atkinson and Maier compared gradual removal of worked solution steps with example/problem pairs in field and lab work; benefits were strongest for near transfer. **Inference:** Offer worked traces, then completion tasks, then independent fresh problems; require explanation of why each step applies. **Limit:** These experiments do not establish far DSA transfer. [Primary conference paper](https://escholarship.org/content/qt81b9j9hs/qt81b9j9hs_noSplash_ef3e30a960524adc5157ece2d6fa3130.pdf).

8. **Fact: reviewed educational guidance supports spacing, alternating worked examples with problems and retrieval.** The US IES guide explicitly grades the evidence for its recommendations; spacing and example/problem interleaving receive moderate evidence. **Inference:** Mix related mastered techniques so the learner must choose a strategy, while protecting novices from excessive task switching. **Limit:** Alternating examples/problems and interleaving different solution strategies are distinct interventions; neither proves a specific DSA scheduler. [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/1).

## Motivations, obstacles and segment hypotheses

**Fact:** Seven introductory CS students participated in 36 interviews in an ICER study of self-assessment reasoning. The authors describe reasons to normalize programming struggles and emphasize effective practices. **Inference:** Show concrete progress and next actions, avoid ranking a struggling novice as inherently incapable, and separate “needed help today” from identity. **Limit:** This qualitative sample cannot estimate prevalence. [ICER 2024 primary abstract](https://icer2024.acm.org/details/icer-2024-papers/4/Understanding-the-Reasoning-Behind-Students-Self-Assessments-of-Ability-in-Introduct).

**Fact:** A qualitative study of Hacker News discussion found complaints about interview relevance, preparation time, implicit norms, anxiety and frustration. **Inference:** A learner may value a manageable plan and realistic practice more than an enormous bank. **Limit:** This is a self-selected practitioner discussion corpus, not an Indian college survey and not evidence of purchase intent. [Microsoft Research publication](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/hiring-is-broken-what-do-developers-say-about-technical-interviews/).

**Fact: interview setting can change measured performance.** Behroozi et al.'s randomized experiment recruited 50 university undergraduate/graduate students with prior DSA coursework; 48 supplied complete or partial usable data (22 private, 26 public). They solved the same whiteboard task privately or while observed and thinking aloud. Excluding timeouts, median correctness was 3 versus 1 passed test cases; failure rates were 36.3% private versus 61.5% public. **Inference:** Separate private skill checkpoints from interview-format rehearsal; confidence earned privately need not transfer automatically to observed communication. **Limits:** One university, one task, whiteboard conditions, eye-tracker discomfort, limited real job stakes and a non-collaborative interviewer. This is not evidence that CodeWise2 reduces anxiety, doubles interview success or improves placement; the abstract's “more than half” refers to this performance measure, not employability. [Opened author manuscript, ESEC/FSE 2020](https://www.chrisparnin.me/pdf/stress_FSE_20.pdf); published DOI: [10.1145/3368089.3409712](https://doi.org/10.1145/3368089.3409712).

**Fact: direct college DSA evidence identifies conceptual obstacles.** Barzilai et al. observed 13 first-year Information Systems students in an Israeli college using think-aloud on a complex data-structures task during a remotely taught course. Their qualitative analysis identified abstract thinking, flexibility during solving and misconceptions/basic problem understanding as factors associated with solution quality. **Inference:** Diagnose problem interpretation and structure selection separately from syntax; ask students to explain the structure's operations before implementing it. **Limits:** One course, one task, small qualitative case study, not Indian placement candidates; no population prevalence or subscription demand follows. This supports testing abstraction and misconception feedback, not declaring all failures to be lack of practice. [Opened primary paper, JISE 2024](https://jise.org/Volume35/n2/JISE2024v35n2pp175-188.pdf).

**Initial segment hypothesis:** Indian college students approaching placement who already know basic syntax but cannot reliably select and execute DSA approaches. Proposed jobs: know what to do next, retain patterns between sessions, learn from mistakes, and establish whether assisted successes survive a fresh timed attempt. Proposed obstacles: crowded resource choices, tutorial fluency mistaken for skill, weak prerequisites, inconsistent practice, language friction, time pressure and fear of failure. These are interview prompts and experiment hypotheses, not measured population findings.

**Global comparison hypothesis:** Introductory CS learners may prioritize course understanding and debugging; career changers may prioritize a bounded plan and visible competence; experienced interview candidates may prioritize speed, strategy selection and realistic communication. Validate each separately. Indian placement context does not imply the same buyers, language needs, interview styles or payment behavior globally.

**Fact/caution:** India Skills Report 2025 evaluates a large test-taking candidate population and defines employability around Global Employability Test results. It is useful context for skills assessment, not a representative estimate of all Indian students, DSA readiness, jobs secured, addressable subscribers or willingness to pay. The 2026 report search result appeared but its PDF could not be opened; no 2026 numerical claim is adopted. [Opened provider report 2025](https://wheebox.com/assets/pdf/ISR_Report_2025.pdf). No credible direct purchasing evidence was established in this brief.

## Mechanism and proof standard

**Proposed mechanism:** Diagnose a prerequisite → attempt a bounded problem → provide the smallest useful hint or faded example → label assistance → explain the approach → solve a genuinely fresh unaided checkpoint → revisit later through retrieval and mixed strategy selection. Keep support available for learning; make assessment conditions explicit. Do not punish help-seeking or represent an assisted pass as independent mastery.

**Proposed primary outcome:** Baseline-adjusted performance on timed, fresh, unassisted problems at a prespecified delayed test. Record correctness, time, valid complexity reasoning and ability to explain/modify the solution. Separate immediate near transfer, delayed near transfer and farther strategy-selection transfer. Fresh means unseen statement/solution, not just different variable names or inputs. Keep assessment items out of training and AI retrieval; independently review matching difficulty and checker validity.

**Near versus far transfer:** A new problem using the recently practiced structure and closely matched solution schema is near transfer even if its story changes. Choosing and combining strategies in a different context, without a topic label, is farther transfer. Observed live explanation adds another setting change. Report these separately: passing a same-step checkpoint cannot establish interview readiness or general problem-solving competence.

**Credible pilot:** Recruit consenting target learners; preregister outcomes and time budget; compare against equal-time ordinary bank/editorial practice; stratify by baseline ability and language; randomize where feasible; include all assigned learners, attrition and uncertainty. Measure retention after at least a week and later again, as well as assistance usage, confidence calibration and adverse frustration. Log minimal consented metadata rather than private code or AI reasoning. Freeze and hash content/checkers for evidence; changed suites invalidate related results. No learner code on the host.

**Confidence ethics:** Claim “see what you can do independently” before efficacy evidence. After evidence, report the studied group, comparator, duration and effect with uncertainty. Confidence ratings are secondary: greater confidence without improved fresh performance can be harmful miscalibration. Do not claim anxiety treatment, guaranteed placement, scientifically optimal scheduling, or proven DSA mastery from completion/streak counts. Payment proof requires actual voluntary purchase/renewal behavior in a separately authorized test, not enthusiasm or an employability percentage.
