# CodeWise2: buyers, payment and renewal validation

Research accessed 2026-10-12. Desk research only: no interviews, outreach, transactions or runtime tests were performed. Prices below are experiment hypotheses, not measured willingness to pay. App capabilities are supplied coordinator context; this document does not independently verify them.

## Commercial conclusion

Test a **four-week placement-practice cohort that demonstrates independent problem solving**, with fresh blind assessments, a small accountable group and human-reviewed interview explanations. The downloadable practice app can remain free. Charging merely for 243 problems, AI hints or a study schedule is vulnerable to the public clone, free resources and general AI. A defensible paid service must continuously deliver work that copying the app does not reproduce: new assessment forms, trustworthy scoring, feedback and dependable facilitation.

The likely need is finite, tied to internship/placement/interview windows. A successful learner graduating or getting hired is a good outcome, even if they stop paying. Start with a fixed cohort purchase and explicitly optional next block; do not assume a perpetual subscription. No placement guarantee, accredited certification or employer recognition is established.

## Buyer hypotheses: validate separately

| Buyer / user | Proposed job and trigger | Payment and renewal hypothesis | Main disconfirmation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indian college student / same user | Placement test in 4–12 weeks; knows topics but cannot solve unfamiliar tasks without hints | Pays for proof of improvement, explanation feedback and a group that actually meets. Next block only for a remaining skill gap or another interview window. | Free practice already works; schedule conflict; setup burden; improvement absent. |
| Parent payer / student user | Wants a credible, bounded preparation plan | May fund a fixed cohort with a plain progress report, with the learner's consent. | Parent interest masks an unwilling learner; purchasing depends on false job guarantees. |

| Employer / candidate or junior employee | Needs role-relevant independent ability, explanation and training-gap evidence | Could pay for a separately validated assessment or onboarding service. | DSA irrelevant to role; evidence is easy to game; legal/security review fails. | Global individual / same user | Active technical interviews; comparable finite need | Can test the same service proposition locally, with a separate currency/price study. | Competition, timezone/language support, unfamiliar brand, unavailable platform. ## Rank the avenues

1. **Fresh blind assessments + interview explanation review:** strongest differentiating hypothesis. Public solutions make existing-bank completion weak evidence of transfer. Paid assessments require genuinely new forms, reviewed rubrics and secure handling; they are proposed services, not existing verified capabilities. A fresh problem can still be AI-assisted remotely: describe the actual supervision and evidence limits.
2. **Small cohort accountability:** plausible delivery advantage if participants value a facilitator and dependable peers. Reminders alone are insufficient evidence of learning; a large randomized college-support study found no improvement in grades, credits or retention from low-touch scheduling/reminder/coaching interventions. [Research abstract; search result accessible, direct page returned 403](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25036). Test assessed performance, not streaks.
3. **College evidence:** potentially stronger repeat buyer than the same student indefinitely. Requires faculty acceptance of the rubric, privacy consent, actual budget ownership and a useful action from the report; a dashboard alone has no proven procurement value.
4. **Local privacy and portability:** supporting reason to choose, weak standalone subscription hypothesis. BYOK tutoring still sends selected material to an external provider. Local app does not mean fully offline AI; current provider validation and cross-platform support remain unverified. Platform setup can erase the benefit.

## Lean validation protocol — proposed, not executed

**Stage 1: 12 short interviews.** Recruit 6 placement-window students (include people who abandoned paid tools), 2 parents, 3 placement/faculty budget stakeholders and 1 hiring practitioner. Convenience sampling identifies problems; it cannot estimate the Indian market. Ask one question at a time, before showing the concept:

- “Walk me through your last preparation session and the last unfamiliar problem you attempted.”
- “What did you do when you got stuck, and what happened at the next test?”
- “What have you paid for in the past six months? Who chose and paid? What did you stop using?” Ask permission for a redacted receipt; do not collect banking data.
- “When did a progress report last change a teaching or hiring decision?”
- “How does a training purchase get approved? Which budget, approver and decision date?”
- After a neutral demo: “What would you replace with this? What would prevent you starting?” Avoid “Would accountability help?” and “Would you pay ₹499?”

Proceed to offer tests only if ≥4 of 6 students describe a recent concrete independence/consistency problem and ≥3 have a real upcoming preparation deadline. For colleges, require ≥2 of 3 to identify an actual budget route and a report-driven decision. Otherwise narrow the segment or stop that avenue. These are management gates, not statistically validated benchmarks.

**Stage 2: two truthful messages, same real offer.** A: “Practice consistently with a four-week small group.” B: “Find whether you can solve a new problem without hints.” Show free-clone alternative, exact dates, platform limits, human support, total price and refund terms. Randomize order in interviews, then test balanced traffic with matched audiences. Record qualified visit → booked diagnostic → attended → real paid pilot, including channel and reasons for refusal. Likes and email signups are weak signals. With 20 qualified prospects per message, use results only directionally; do not declare statistical superiority. Proceed if ≥5 of 40 buy at a published price, including ≥3 outside friends/family. Stop/rework if <3 buy or buying mainly requires a guarantee. No fake payment page, fictitious scarcity or invented testimonials.

**Stage 3: one real 10-person, four-week pilot.** Hypotheses for India: ₹499–₹999 per learner for group delivery (two price cells across separate comparable runs); ₹1,499–₹2,499 only if a clearly scoped individual mock/review is included. A 20–30-student college pilot could test ₹10,000–₹20,000 for facilitation and an aggregate report. These are bounded test ranges, not validated market prices. First trial excludes automatic renewal and unlimited AI; disclose BYOK/API expense separately. Teach/support manually before building a commercial platform. If supported devices are unavailable, recruit only supported users or pause; never imply broad compatibility.

At ₹799 × 10, revenue is ₹7,990 before tax, fees and refunds. A assumed four facilitator hours/week × four weeks at ₹300/hour costs ₹4,800, leaving ₹3,190 before assessment authoring, onboarding, acquisition and other costs. Record actual time; low-price cohorts are unlikely to fund individual tutoring. Proceed commercially only if delivery and acquisition costs fit an explicitly documented contribution model.

Measure a no-AI/no-hint baseline, week-four fresh parallel assessment and delayed assessment two weeks later. Predeclare a 0–10 rubric (correctness 0–4, reasoning 0–3, complexity 0–2, edge cases 0–1); blind the scorer to timepoint when practical. Same concepts, different surface details and comparable difficulty; do not reuse exposed public references. Score first attempts and assistance separately. A pre/post pilot without a comparison group cannot establish causality; add a randomized waitlist in a later adequately powered study.

Pilot gate: ≥7/10 complete the final assessment; ≥6/10 improve ≥2 rubric points; median delayed score retains ≥80% of final score; no material integrity/privacy incident; actual delivery stays within budget. Missing assessments count as missing and as noncompletion, never zero skill. These exploratory thresholds require rubric calibration, not claims of validated employability.

**Renewal test:** after results, show an optional next block with a concrete remaining goal and unchanged disclosed price. Among learners who still need preparation, seek ≥40% actual repurchase within 14 days; report numerator/denominator and separately report successful exits, pauses and dissatisfied churn. If purchases occur but learning does not improve, stop the outcome claim and revise delivery. If learning improves but few eligible learners repurchase, retain a finite cohort model rather than inventing recurring value.

## Ethical offer and evidence handling

Show all charges and API-cost responsibilities before payment. Explicit refund proposal: full refund before the first session, and within seven days of start for mismatch; refund promptly if a promised service cannot be delivered. Publish exact request method and processing window and budget the liability. Any later subscription needs affirmative consent, clear next billing date, a reminder and simple cancellation; cancelled users keep export access. FTC identifies misleading urgency, concealed fees and obstructive cancellation as dark patterns. [FTC report summary](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers). India's CCPA notified dark-pattern guidelines on 30 November 2023; this is a design reference, not legal-clearance advice. [Government notice](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1983994&lang=2&noshow=1&reg=3).

Never imply accredited certificates, placement probability or validated hiring readiness. Give a factual assessment report with limitations. Store interview recordings, identities, receipts and learner exports outside source/build/version control; this research file contains no participant data. Obtain separate consent for parent/college sharing, retain only required fields, and publish no learner data without explicit authorization.
